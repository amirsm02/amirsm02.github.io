Biology / Protein design

A generated protein that fluoresces in the lab

The paper

Simulating 500 million years of evolution with a language model ↗

Thomas Hayes and colleagues

ESM3 helped generate esmGFP, a fluorescent protein with 58% sequence identity to the closest known fluorescent protein in the authors' comparison.

Published · laboratory evidencePublished 16 Jan 2025Sources checked 22 Oct 2026

01

What changed?

Starting point

Natural and engineered fluorescent proteins already existed; preserving function far from their sequences remained the design challenge.

The reported result

A model-generated candidate fluoresced despite substantial sequence divergence.

The conditions

A selected design tested under the authors' assay conditions; 58% depends on the reference set and alignment.

The limit of the claim

“500 million years” estimates sequence divergence. It is neither runtime nor a replay of evolution.

Source for the comparison ↗
02

What did the AI do?

ESM3

Generated protein candidates from partial sequence and structural prompts, then variants of a promising candidate.

Researchers

Researchers set the target, selected candidates, synthesized proteins, measured fluorescence, and analyzed sequence distance.

What is disclosed: The paper describes a model-and-laboratory workflow. The authors released a smaller open model, not all model sizes.
